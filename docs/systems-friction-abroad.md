# Systems friction abroad

**Status:** Essay outline · Spring 2026 reflection  
**Audience:** Informatics students, public-interest builders, anyone who has been the person standing at the wrong counter

## Thesis

Study abroad is often sold as culture and cuisine. The part that stuck for me was **systems friction**: the gap between how a city presents itself to tourists and how it actually runs for someone who has to register, pay, schedule, and prove they belong.

That friction is not a personal failure. It is a design signal — usually invisible to people who already know the steps.

## What “friction” means here

Not annoyance for its own sake. Specific patterns:

1. **Assumed local context** — You are expected to already have an address history, a bank relationship, or a national ID workflow you have never used.
2. **Unwritten sequencing** — The app or office works only if you did step B before step A, but nothing on the page says so.
3. **Single-language defaults** — Spanish-only flows with no plain-language fallback; English “support” that is a PDF, not an interface.
4. **Hours and channels that don’t match student life** — In-person only, narrow windows, no status tracking after you submit.
5. **Error messages that blame the user** — “Invalid document” instead of “we need a different scan format.”

These are the same failure modes I watch for at ServeIT: sites and tools built for the maintainer’s mental model, not the visitor’s first visit.

## Madrid as a systems lab (not a postcard)

Spring 2026 at Complutense was a semester abroad through IU Overseas Study — EU politics, Spanish, strategic management, plus living in the city day to day. The coursework gave vocabulary for **institutional design** (EU governance, policy layers, organizational strategy). Daily life gave **ground truth** for how those institutions meet a person at a counter or on a phone screen.

I am not documenting private administrative details here. The point is the **pattern library**:

| Layer | Question I kept asking |
|-------|------------------------|
| Civic / government-facing | Who is this form written for? What if you are new? |
| University | How does an international student discover the one office that actually handles their case? |
| Commercial apps | What breaks when your phone number or payment profile is from another country? |
| Transit & utilities | Where does the “official” app end and word-of-mouth begin? |

## Connection to Informatics

Informatics is not only building features. It is modeling **who the system excludes by default** and whether that exclusion is intentional, negligent, or never questioned.

Abroad makes exclusion legible because you feel it in real time. At home, the same gaps often hide behind familiarity — you learned the workaround in high school or from a parent.

## Restaurant-floor parallel (brief)

I grew up in a family restaurant. A system either survives contact with a real customer or it doesn’t. Study abroad reminded me that **public services are customer-facing systems too** — just with higher stakes and worse error UX.
