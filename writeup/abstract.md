# Abstract (draft · 1 page target · no fake results)

**Title (working):** Systems Friction as a First-Class Design Requirement for Outsiders in Civic Digital Services

**Status:** Draft abstract for a future 4–8 page research note. Reflective practice; no controlled experiment results.

Public and civic digital services often assume local context, language fluency, and unwritten process knowledge. For outsiders ... non-native speakers, new arrivals, and first-time users ... that assumption shows up as systems friction: stalled forms, PDF-only “language support,” blaming error messages, and channels that do not match outsider schedules. This research note locks a falsifiable claim that treating such friction as a first-class design requirement, rather than a post-launch accessibility fix, supports more accountable AI and digital public services. Method: reflective pattern extraction from a Spring 2026 semester abroad in Madrid (Complutense / IU Overseas Study), documented as a public-safe pattern library, plus a reusable Outsider Impact Review one-pager for builders. Contribution at this stage is the claim, the patterns, the artifact template, and explicit limitations (single observer, one city, no controlled trial). Closest prior work on migrant administrative literacy, language barriers in e-government, and linguistic fairness in AI documents related harms but does not fully treat outsider friction as a pre-automation design gate for public-interest AI. No quantitative validation is claimed here; a minimal kill test (dual review of two civic flows) is specified for later work.

**Keywords:** systems friction; digital government; language access; outsider users; public-interest AI; reflective practice
