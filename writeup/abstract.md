# Abstract (draft · 1 page target · no fake results)

**Title (working):** Systems Friction as a First-Class Design Requirement for Outsiders in Civic Digital Services

**Status:** Matches `writeup/systems-friction-writeup.tex` (Aug 2026). Reflective practice; no controlled experiment results.

Civic and public digital services routinely assume local context, language fluency, and unwritten process knowledge. For outsiders (non-native speakers, new arrivals, and first-time users), that assumption appears as *systems friction*: stalled forms, PDF-only “language support,” blaming error messages, and channels that do not match outsider schedules. This independent research note locks a falsifiable claim: treating outsider friction as a first-class design requirement, rather than a post-launch accessibility patch, is a necessary input for accountable civic digital services and public-interest automation. Method: reflective pattern extraction from a Spring 2026 semester abroad in Madrid (Complutense / IU Overseas Study), recorded as a public-safe pattern library in the `madrid-ai-ethics` repository, plus a reusable Outsider Impact Review one-pager for builders. Contribution at this stage is the claim, five observed patterns, the review artifact, and explicit limits (single observer, one city, reflective method, no controlled trial). Adjacent literature on administrative literacy, administrative burden, and WCAG baselines documents related harms; the operational gap targeted here is a builder-facing pre-automation gate. No quantitative validation is claimed. A minimal dual-review kill test is specified for later work. This PDF is a portfolio-style writeup, not a peer-reviewed publication and not IU or clinic research output.

**Keywords:** systems friction; digital government; language access; outsider users; public-interest AI; reflective practice
