## Closest works (seed)

### 1. Administrative literacy in the digital welfare state (migrants, Finland)

Buchert et al. / related Cambridge work on migrants navigating digital public services in Finland (administrative literacy).  
**Why close:** Documents language + structural knowledge burdens for migrants in e-government.  
**Does not fully answer this claim:** Focuses on literacy/resources of claimants more than a builder-facing requirement that outsider friction gate AI/automation before launch.

### 2. Generative AI for language/administrative barriers (immigrants, public services)

Recent work (e.g. Norwegian public-service context) on immigrants using generative tools to translate/simplify bureaucracy.  
**Why close:** Ties language barriers, admin literacy, and AI assistance.  
**Does not fully answer:** Often studies user-side workarounds; less a design-time Outsider Impact Review that can kill a ship decision when baseline access fails.

### 3. Critical interface analysis of immigration / civic websites (e.g. USCIS-style analyses)

TPC / critical interface work on government sites excluding diverse language users.  
**Why close:** Interface-level exclusion and linguistic justice.  
**Does not fully answer:** Usually site critique, not a portable pre-automation checklist for public-interest AI accountability.

### 4. Co-design with migrants for easier access to public services (e.g. easyRights / H2020-style)

Co-design / hackathon approaches to reduce bureaucratic and idiomatic barriers.  
**Why close:** Participatory fixes for migrant access.  
**Does not fully answer:** Process contribution differs from falsifiable claim + kill-test artifact framed for AI/civic builders.

### 5. Non-native speaker webpage heuristics (thesis / heuristic evaluation work)

Heuristics aimed at usability issues when sites are not in the user’s language.  
**Why close:** Evaluation method for non-native users.  
**Does not fully answer:** Heuristic sets ≠ treating friction as first-class requirement specifically for accountable public AI.

### 6. Linguistic pluralism as algorithmic fairness

Work arguing language is a core fairness dimension for NLP/AI (benchmarks often English-first).  
**Why close:** FAccT-adjacent; language as fairness.  
**Does not fully answer:** Model/benchmark fairness ≠ civic service flow design for first-time outsiders at the counter/app.

### 7. Procedural fairness + public engagement in ADM

Ethics/IT work on procedural fairness and public engagement in algorithmic decision-making.  
**Why close:** Accountability, contestation, engagement.  
**Does not fully answer:** Rarely centers lived outsider friction patterns from study-abroad reflective practice as the design gate.

### 8. EU / policy guidance on AI and algorithm-driven discrimination (public sector)

**Why close:** Public AI accountability vocabulary.  
**Does not fully answer:** Policy guidance ≠ operational one-pager builders use before shipping clinic/civic automation.
